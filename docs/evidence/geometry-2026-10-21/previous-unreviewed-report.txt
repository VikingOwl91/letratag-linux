# Resolution investigation, 2026-10-21: open hardware finding

Status: **OPEN.** The across-tape pitch is verified (160 dpi, stable). The
feed pitch is not: 160 dpi on 2026-10-20 vs ~305-316 dpi on 2026-10-21
(reproduced three times, battery-independent). The 200 dpi suggestion is
refuted by measurement. The trigger of the feed-pitch change is
unidentified. No rendering-pipeline change was made and no unverified
workaround was introduced.

## Question

Is the DYMO LetraTag 200B's resolution 160 dpi (as previously measured),
200 dpi (as suggested by the S2 device file), or something else — and is
the apparent feed-resolution change an actual DPI change, or a feed
stepping / raster interpretation behavior?

## The 200 dpi suggestion is refuted

Calibration measurements (analog calipers, 0.05 mm; images; three
reproduced prints):

| Feature | Dots | Measured | 200 dpi predicts | 160 dpi predicts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tick spacing (feed) | 63 | 5.25 mm | 8.0 mm | 10.0 mm |
| Rectangle width (feed) | 252 | 20.25 mm | 32.0 mm | 40.0 mm |
| Rectangle height (across) | 30 | 4.75 mm | 3.81 mm | 4.76 mm |

200 dpi matches none of the measurements. S2's `dpi: 200` (device file
`LT_200B.json5`) is its engine's mm-to-dots rendering conversion — an
assumption, not a verified device pitch; it matches neither the 2026-10-20
nor the 2026-10-21 measurements.

## Verified: across pitch is 160 dpi, stable

* 2026-10-20: 16 ink rows = 2.25-2.5 mm (2.54 mm expected at 160 dpi).
* 2026-10-21: rectangle height 30 dots = 4.75 mm (4.76 mm at 160 dpi).
* The wire frame is 32 dots; 30 rows print (S1: "the printer can actually
  print 30 pixels in height"; S2: `PROTOCOL_HEAD_FRAME = 32`,
  `PRINTABLE_DOTS = 30`). At 160 dpi the printed band is 5.08 mm, centered
  on the 12 mm tape; the 30-row content area is 4.76 mm.

## The feed pitch conflict

* **2026-10-20** (attempt 7 + batch A/B; fresh batteries; 400-feed-step
  rasters; ruler, multi-segment, internally consistent): 152 ink dots =
  24.30 mm = **158.9 dpi**; 400 dots = 66.55 mm total (~160 dpi).
* **2026-10-21** (calibration prints; 315-feed-step rasters; calipers +
  images; reproduced three times): 252-dot rectangle = 20.25 mm
  (**~316 dpi**), 63-dot ticks = 5.25 mm (**~305 dpi**), 30-dot height =
  4.75 mm (160 dpi across), total 43.15 mm, lead-in 10 mm.
* **Battery ruled out:** a fresh-battery print (rechargeable cells at 100%)
  reproduced the ~305-316 dpi feed pitch exactly.
* 160 dpi feed is *impossible* for today's prints: 315 dots at 160 dpi =
  50.0 mm of content cannot fit in the 43.15 mm label.

## Mechanism analysis

* **Protocol commands: identical.** Both sessions use byte-identical
  command structure (`ESC s` preamble; `ESC D` with `bits_per_pixel=1`,
  `alignment=0x02`, u32 feed count, u32 height 32; `ESC E`; `ESC A`;
  `ESC Q`). The only protocol-level difference is the feed count
  (400 on 10-20, 315 on 10-21). S2 documents the width field as
  `feed_count` (the number of feed steps).
* **Mechanical behavior: ruled out.** Roller slip or gear skipping would
  produce non-uniform spacing; today's output is a uniform 2:1 horizontal
  compression (rectangle width halved, tick spacing halved, height
  unchanged, ticks mutually consistent).
* **Feed stepping vs raster interpretation: both consistent.** The feed
  step size halved (0.15875 mm to 0.0804 mm per feed step) at a similar
  step rate (~51-55 steps/s): half-step micro-stepping, or equivalently
  one raster column per half-step. The raster sent is verified correct
  (mock captures decode to the exact pattern), so the difference is in the
  printer's feed stepping or its interpretation of the feed count — not in
  our encoding.
* **Trigger: unidentified.** The only protocol-level correlate is the feed
  count (400 vs 315). A feed-count threshold (width-dependent stepping) and
  a persistent device-state change between the sessions are both possible;
  they are confounded (all 10-20 prints were 400-step, all of today's are
  315-step). The battery is ruled out.

## The 400-column test was not achieved: unintended CUPS scaling

The approved final test was one 400-feed-step calibration print (63.5 mm
x 12 mm page = exactly 400 px at 160 dpi; media length 6350). The PDF was
verified offline to render to 400 columns (mock capture: width 400,
rectangle 252 dots at columns 10..262, ticks every 63 dots, band rows
1..30). However, the transmitted raster was **315 columns** (job
`letratag-e2e-48`: 7 writes, final 82 bytes = legacy body 1284 =
width 315). An offline reproduction through CUPS with the identical
command (`lp -o media=custom_12x63.5mm`) produces a 315-column raster
with the pattern scaled 0.787x (rectangle ~199 dots, ink rows 4..27):
**CUPS scaled the 63.5 mm page to the default 50 mm media** — unintended
scaling by the scheduler, the exact failure mode the CUPS integration must
avoid. The custom media length was not honored by the everywhere PPD.

Consequences: the 400-feed-step raster never reached the printer, so the
width-dependence hypothesis is untested. The printed label is the
calibration pattern scaled to 315 columns (rectangle ~199 dots feed,
~24 dots across).

## Open finding

**The feed pitch of the LetraTag 200B is not fixed at 160 dpi: it is
~305-316 dpi in the current device state (reproduced, battery-independent)
and was 160 dpi on 2026-10-20. The trigger — a feed-count threshold
(width-dependent stepping) or a persistent device-state change — is
unidentified because feed count and session date are confounded, and the
400-feed-step test print was invalidated by unintended CUPS scaling.**

Until the trigger is identified, a single fixed layout dpi cannot be
correct in both states, and no anisotropic resolution should be
advertised. Recommended follow-ups (each needs explicit operator approval):

1. Map the CUPS media handling so non-default label lengths reach the
   printer unscaled (the everywhere PPD currently substitutes the default
   50 mm media and scales the page) — a CUPS integration fix, not a
   resolution change.
2. One 400-feed-step calibration print through a path that provably
   transmits 400 columns (e.g. the `hardware-send` path with a 400-column
   raster, bypassing the CUPS media substitution), to test
   width-dependence directly.
3. If width-dependent: map the threshold and represent the piecewise feed
   pitch explicitly. If state-dependent: identify the state (cassette,
   tape roll, firmware counter) and document it.
